# When Should You Replace a Toilet Instead of Repairing It?

Most toilet problems can be repaired.

A running toilet may only need a flapper or fill valve. A loose handle is usually simple. So we do not recommend replacing a toilet just because it is old.

But there are times when replacement makes more sense.

## Repeated clogs

An occasional clog is normal.

A toilet that clogs regularly, especially when the drain line itself is fine, may simply have poor flushing performance.

Modern toilets can use much less water and still flush very well.

## Cracks or leaks

If the porcelain tank or bowl is cracked, replacement is usually the safest option.

A toilet that rocks or leaks around the base should also be checked. Sometimes the issue is only a failed seal or flange, but repeated leaking can be a good reason to replace the toilet at the same time.

## Too many repairs

Replacing a flapper or fill valve makes sense.

Replacing parts over and over on a toilet that still flushes poorly does not.

At some point, putting the money toward a better toilet is the smarter choice.

## Older toilets can use much more water

Very old toilets may use 3.5 gallons per flush or more.

Modern WaterSense toilets use 1.28 gallons or less while still meeting flushing-performance standards.

If an older toilet works perfectly, there is no urgent reason to replace it. But if it is already causing problems, improved water efficiency is another benefit of upgrading.

## Comfort matters too

Some homeowners replace a perfectly functional toilet because they want a taller comfort-height model or an elongated bowl.

If you are already remodeling or replacing an older toilet, it can be worth choosing something more comfortable.

## Repair or replace?

Our rule is simple:

If it is a good toilet and needs an inexpensive part, repair it.

If it constantly clogs, leaks, rocks, flushes poorly, is cracked, or keeps needing repairs, replacement probably makes more sense.
