# Is the Bay Area Banning Gas Water Heaters? What Homeowners Need to Know

There has been a lot of confusing information about whether gas water heaters are being "banned" in the Bay Area.

The short answer is:

**Your existing gas water heater is not being banned, and you do not have to remove a working unit.**

The new rules affect replacement equipment over time.

That distinction is important.

## What did the Bay Area actually adopt?

In 2023, the Bay Area Air Quality Management District adopted new zero-NOx standards for water heaters.

Under the rule currently on the books:

- Smaller water heaters under 75,000 BTU per hour, which includes most standard residential gas tank water heaters, are scheduled to become subject to the zero-NOx standard beginning January 1, 2027.
- Larger water heaters from 75,000 BTU up to 2 million BTU per hour, including most gas tankless water heaters, are scheduled to become subject to the standard beginning January 1, 2031.

The rule applies to newly manufactured equipment. It does not require homeowners to remove an existing working gas water heater.

## But the 2027 date may move to 2028

This is where a lot of the current confusion comes from.

In July 2026, the Air District released proposed amendments that would delay the small-water-heater requirement from January 2027 until January 2028.

The proposal also includes exemptions for some situations where electrical capacity, space constraints, or financial hardship would make conversion particularly difficult.

As of now, those amendments have **not yet been adopted**.

The Air District's Board is tentatively scheduled to consider them in November 2026.

So for now:

**2027 is still the adopted date.  
2028 is the proposed new date.**

We would not tell a homeowner that the rule has officially been delayed until the Board actually adopts the amendment.

## Does this mean you have to switch to a heat pump water heater?

Not today.

If your existing gas water heater is working, you can keep using it.

If it fails before the applicable rule takes effect, a conventional gas replacement may still be available.

After the zero-NOx requirement applies, the available replacement options will depend on the rules in effect at that time, any exemptions, and what compliant equipment is available on the market.

Today, heat pump water heaters are the main residential zero-NOx option.

That does not necessarily mean every Bay Area home will be forced into the exact same solution.

The proposed amendments are specifically trying to address homes where electrification is unusually difficult or expensive.

## What about gas tankless water heaters?

Tankless has a different timeline.

Most residential gas tankless systems are rated above 75,000 BTU per hour, which puts them in the category currently scheduled for zero-NOx requirements beginning January 1, 2031.

That matters if you are thinking about spending several thousand dollars converting from a gas tank to gas tankless today.

You may only get one equipment cycle out of that conversion before the replacement rules change.

That is one reason Water Heater Boys generally does not push homeowners toward expensive gas tankless conversions right now unless there is a specific reason they really want tankless.

## Should you replace your gas water heater early?

Usually, we would not replace a perfectly good water heater solely because a regulation is coming.

But planning ahead is smart.

If your gas tank is already getting old, it is worth understanding your options before it fails.

A heat pump conversion can require electrical work, condensate drainage, and enough physical space and airflow for the new heater.

Those are easier decisions to make when you are not standing in a garage with a leaking tank and no hot water.

## What happens if my water heater fails after the rule changes?

That will depend on the final version of the rule and your particular home.

Some houses will be relatively easy to convert to a heat pump water heater.

Others may need substantial electrical work or have physical limitations that make the conversion more difficult.

The Air District's proposed 2026 amendments are intended to create more flexibility for some of those situations.

That is why we think homeowners should be cautious about headlines that say simply, "Gas water heaters are banned."

The actual rule is more complicated than that.

## What should Bay Area homeowners do now?

For most homeowners, there is no reason to panic or replace a working water heater.

The practical approach is:

Know how old your current heater is.

If it is getting toward the end of its useful life, understand what a gas replacement and a heat pump conversion would each involve.

If you are considering an expensive gas tankless conversion, factor the 2031 rules into the decision.

And when replacement time comes, check the rules that are actually in effect at that time.
